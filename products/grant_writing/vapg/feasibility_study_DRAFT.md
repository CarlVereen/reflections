## 1. Executive Summary

Howard's Orchard proposes to expand its existing value-added line — jams and
jellies — into **pressed cider, dried fruit, and baked pies** made from fruit
grown on its own pesticide-free orchard in Catalina, Arizona. The venture
converts raw fruit (which is perishable, seasonal, and low-margin) into
shelf-stable, higher-margin branded products sold year-round through the farm's
established U-pick channel, farmers markets, and online.

The venture is **technically feasible** (proven processes, modest equipment),
**market feasible** (strong and growing demand for local, organic, farm-branded
foods in the Tucson metro), and **financially feasible** at a small scale with
conservative assumptions. It directly increases farm income and extends the
selling season beyond the current July–November window.

## 2. Technical / Operational Feasibility

**Current state:** ~1 acre in production, ~220 fruit trees (apples, peaches,
pears; pecans/walnuts), expandable to 1.5 acres. Existing jam/jelly production.
U-pick sales July–November. Pesticide-free / organic practices (certification
pending via AZ cost-share).

**Proposed products and process:**
| Product | Source fruit | Process | Equipment needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressed cider | apples (culls + surplus) | wash → press → pasteurize → bottle | cider press, pasteurizer, bottling 【CONFIRM】 |
| Dried fruit | apples, peaches, pears | slice → dehydrate → package | commercial dehydrator(s), sealer 【CONFIRM】 |
| Pies | apples, peaches, pears | bake → box (or par-bake/freeze) | licensed kitchen, oven, freezer 【CONFIRM】 |
| Jams/jellies (existing) | all fruit | cook → jar | (in place) |

**Key advantage — using culls:** value-added lets you sell "seconds" (bruised or
undersized fruit unsellable as U-pick) that are otherwise a total loss. This
raises revenue per tree without growing a single additional apple.

**Capacity:** small-batch, owner-operated. 【CONFIRM: target units/week per
product】. Production scales with the season and can use frozen/dried inventory to
sell in the off-season.

**Regulatory path (must confirm):**
- Jams, jellies, dried fruit: likely **AZ Cottage Food** eligible (home kitchen).
- Pies, pressed cider (juice): typically require a **commercial/licensed kitchen**
  and food-safety plan.
- Hard cider (if pursued): separate **TTB + AZ liquor licensing** — treat as a
  later, separate track.

**Conclusion:** Technically feasible with proven methods and modest, off-the-shelf
equipment. Primary gating item is kitchen/licensing for pies and cider.

## 3. Market Feasibility

**Demand drivers:**
- Strong, growing consumer demand for **local, organic, pesticide-free** food.
- Tucson metro population ~1,000,000 within ~30–40 min — a large, reachable market.
- The farm already has a **loyal U-pick customer base** driving in from across
  the state July–November — a warm audience for branded take-home products.
- **Agritourism halo:** people who pick their own fruit buy value-added products
  at the point of experience (impulse + gift purchases) at premium prices.

**Target customers:**
1. Existing U-pick visitors (highest conversion, zero acquisition cost).
2. Tucson-area farmers-market & local-food shoppers.
3. Local specialty grocers / gift shops (wholesale, later).
4. Online / seasonal gift buyers (dried fruit and cider ship well).

**Competition & differentiation:** Regional orchards and grocery brands exist,
but Howard's differentiates on **pesticide-free/organic, single-farm identity, a
45-year local story, and the U-pick experience** — advantages a grocery brand
cannot copy. 【CONFIRM: list 2–3 local competitors and their price points】
