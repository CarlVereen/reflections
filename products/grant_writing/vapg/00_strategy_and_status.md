# VAPG — Strategy & Status
### Howard's Orchard LLC · value-added venture (jams → cider, pies, dried fruit)

## The strategy (why we're doing it this way)

- **Grant type: Working Capital, SIMPLIFIED application, request UNDER $50,000.**
  Why: working-capital requests **≥ $50k require an independent third-party
  feasibility study**; the **simplified application (under $50k) is exempt.** At
  your scale, a right-sized ask ~$20–35k avoids that cost/complexity and is far
  more winnable.
- **Match:** VAPG requires a **1:1 match** (your dollars/in-kind = the grant
  amount). We size the ask to a match you can actually document (cash on hand +
  eligible in-kind like your own labor and equipment). Better to request $25k we
  can match than $75k we can't.
- **Your priority status:** Beginning Farmer + Socially Disadvantaged (Ashley
  51%) → VAPG **reserves funds** for both. Real scoring edge.
- **Reviewers score the NARRATIVE**, not the study. So these drafts exist to (a)
  prove the venture is real and feasible, and (b) feed tight, cited answers into
  the application narrative.

## Value-added qualification (how we meet the definition)
Howard's qualifies two ways at once:
1. **Change in physical state** — fresh fruit → jam, jelly, pressed cider, dried
   fruit, pies.
2. **Differentiated product** — pesticide-free / organically grown, local,
   identity-preserved (a named family farm), sold through U-pick agritourism.

## What I need from you to finalize (rough is fine)
1. **What would the money buy?** Best guesses on: commercial/licensed kitchen or
   shared commissary? cider press? dehydrators? cold storage? packaging/labeling
   equipment? A rough wish-list with ballpark prices lets me finish the budget.
2. **Kitchen situation:** do you make the jams in a home kitchen now, a certified
   kitchen, or rent one? (Affects regulatory path below.)
3. **Cider plan:** sweet/pressed cider (juice) or hard cider (alcohol)? They have
   very different licensing.
4. **How much cash could you put toward the match**, and roughly the value of
   in-kind (your labor, existing equipment, the orchard)?

## Regulatory flags to check (I'll help, but note these)
- **AZ Cottage Food** rules likely cover jams/jellies and dried fruit from a home
  kitchen; **pies** and **pressed cider (juice)** usually need a commercial/
  licensed kitchen and food-safety steps. **Hard cider** needs a TTB + AZ liquor
  license — a separate track.
- Getting **organic certified** (cheap via the AZ cost-share) strengthens the
  "differentiated product" scoring.
