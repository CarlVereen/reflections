### Short business description (~100 words)
> Howard's Orchard is a small, woman-owned family farm in the Arizona desert,
> first planted by Jim Howard and now stewarded by our family. On one irrigated
> acre of about 220 fruit trees, we grow apples, peaches, and pears without any
> pesticides, keep ducks and chickens for eggs, and make small-batch jams and
> jellies. From July through November we open as a U-pick farm so families
> across the state can experience farm life and take home fresh, clean food.
> Our mission is simple: keep this 45-year-old orchard alive and bless our
> community with honest, organically grown food.

### How would you use the grant? (~120 words)
> This grant would fund two priorities that keep our small farm viable. First,
> water: we farm in the drought-stricken desert on a partial drip system, and
> we're investing in more efficient irrigation and rainwater harvesting so every
> gallon goes further. Second, growth: we're expanding our value-added kitchen
> beyond jams and jellies into cider, pies, and dried fruit — products that let
> a one-acre farm earn a real living — and adding hardy new crops like elderberry
> and mulberry that thrive in our climate. A $4,250 grant would go directly into
> irrigation improvements and value-added equipment, helping us serve more
> families and secure the farm for the next generation.

### Why does your business matter / what's your story? (~90 words)
> Forty-five years ago, Jim Howard moved to Arizona for his health, planted a few
> trees, and opened his orchard as "a community project." We now carry that
> torch — raising our family on the same land and inviting our neighbors to pick
> their own fruit every season. In a state where small farms are disappearing,
> we're proving a tiny, pesticide-free family orchard can still feed a community.
> Support helps us protect a piece of living local history and keep it growing.
