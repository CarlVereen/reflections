**Tell us about your business and what you'd do with the grant.**

Howard's Orchard is a family U-pick farm on three and a half acres in Catalina,
Arizona. It was started by Jim Howard, who moved to the desert in the 1970s for
his health, planted his first trees, and in 1984 opened the farm as what he
called "a community project" — a place where neighbors could come pick their own
fruit and taste what real, fresh food is supposed to be. Forty-five years later,
my husband and I now steward that same land, and we are raising our family here
with the same purpose Jim had: to grow clean, pesticide-free food and share farm
life with our community.

We grow apples, peaches, and pears, keep ducks and chickens for eggs, and make
our own jams and jellies. From July through November, families drive in from
across the state to pick their own fruit by the pound. We farm without any
pesticides and follow organic practices because we believe our neighbors deserve
food we would feed our own twelve children — and we do.

Keeping a 45-year-old farm alive in the Arizona desert is not easy, and water is
our hardest challenge. Right now we water with a partial drip system. My dream is
a full rainwater-harvesting and permaculture watering system that would let this
land thrive on far less water and stay productive for the next generation. I also
want to grow our small jam-and-jelly kitchen into cider, pies, and dried fruit,
and add hardy new crops like elderberry and mulberry that suit our climate.

A $10,000 Amber Grant would go straight into that future: improving how we
capture and use water, and expanding the value-added products that let a small
family farm earn a living. Every dollar would help us do what Jim set out to do —
keep this little piece of desert green, and keep blessing our community with good,
honest food.
