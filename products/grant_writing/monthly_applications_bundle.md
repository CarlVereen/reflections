**What they ask:** short answers about your business, your goals, and what the
$500 would do.

**Business (paste):**
> Howard's Orchard is a woman-owned family U-pick fruit farm in Catalina, Arizona.
> On one pesticide-free acre of about 220 trees, we grow apples, peaches, and
> pears, make small-batch jams and jellies, and open the farm July–November so
> families can pick their own fruit. We're carrying on a 45-year-old farm and
> expanding into cider, pies, and dried fruit.

**What would $500 do? (paste):**
> $500 would buy packaging and labeling supplies for our jams and dried fruit so
> we can sell more of what we grow — including fruit that's too bruised for U-pick

> harvest into income for a tiny family farm.

**Your goal (paste):**
> To keep this 45-year-old desert farm alive and self-sustaining — growing clean,
> pesticide-free food, expanding our value-added products, and improving our
> water system so the orchard thrives for the next generation.

## 2) The Pollination Project — $1,000 (community-project angle required)
**Where:** thepollinationproject.org → Apply (rolling; grants awarded daily).
**IMPORTANT:** They fund **community-benefit projects**, not general business
costs. So we pitch a specific *give-back project*, not "fund our farm." Only apply
if you'll actually do the project below (keep it honest).

**Suggested project — "Free Farm Days for local families":**
> Our project offers free U-pick farm days and hands-on growing lessons for
> low-income families and kids in our community, so children who rarely see where
> food comes from can pick fresh fruit, meet the ducks and chickens, and learn how
> a pesticide-free farm works. Howard's Orchard has been a community gathering
> place for 45 years; this project removes the cost barrier so more neighbors can
> take part. The $1,000 would cover free-day supplies, simple educational
> materials for kids, and fruit we set aside to send home with families in need.

**Who it helps / impact (paste):**
> Low-income families and children in the Catalina/Tucson area gain free access to
> a working farm, fresh food, and basic food-growing education — building healthier
> habits and a connection to local agriculture.

*(If you'd rather donate surplus produce to a food bank instead, tell me and I'll
reframe the project around that — either is a genuine, fundable angle.)*

**Where:** search "Venmo Small Business Grant" for the current round + form.
**Eligibility you meet:** fewer than 10 employees AND **under $50,000 annual
revenue** (you're ~$8k — a real edge; most applicants can't meet the revenue cap).

**Your story (paste):**
> Howard's Orchard is a 45-year-old family U-pick farm in the Arizona desert, now
> run by our family and 51% owned by Ashley. On one pesticide-free acre we grow
> apples, peaches, and pears, make jams and jellies, and welcome families to pick
> their own fruit. We're beginning farmers working to keep a piece of local history
> alive and bless our community with clean, honest food.

**How would $20,000 help? (paste):**
> $20,000 would let us expand our value-added kitchen into cider, dried fruit, and
> pies, and improve our desert water system — the two things that would turn our
> small seasonal farm into a year-round, sustainable business. It would fund
> processing equipment (a cider press, dehydrators, packaging) and efficient
> irrigation, helping us earn more from every tree and secure the farm for our
> family's future.
