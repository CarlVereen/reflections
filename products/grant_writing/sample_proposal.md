## 1. Executive Summary

Riverside Youth Collective (RYC) requests $148,500 to expand *Bridge*, our
evidence-informed after-school mentoring program, from 60 to 150 middle-school
youth in the Eastside neighborhood over the next 12 months. Eastside's three
public middle schools report chronic-absenteeism rates above 28% and the
city's highest rate of juvenile justice referrals. *Bridge* pairs each young
person with a trained adult mentor and a structured schedule of academic
support, social-emotional learning, and paid summer work experience. Over the
past three years, 91% of *Bridge* participants advanced to the next grade on
time and none entered the juvenile justice system during program enrollment.
This request funds three additional site coordinators, mentor training, and
the transportation that is the single largest barrier to Eastside families'
participation.

## 2. Statement of Need

Eastside is a community of 22,000 residents where 41% of children live below
the federal poverty line (U.S. Census, 2024 ACS 5-year). The three middle
schools serving the neighborhood — Lincoln, Carver, and Hillside — collectively
enroll 1,840 students, of whom the district identifies 620 as "at elevated
risk" based on absenteeism and disciplinary data. After the 3:00 p.m. bell,
most of these students are unsupervised until early evening; the county
reports that 63% of juvenile offenses committed by this age group occur
between 3:00 and 7:00 p.m. on weekdays. Existing programming reaches fewer than
one in ten eligible youth, and the two nearest alternatives have waitlists
exceeding four months. The gap is not interest — RYC's own waitlist holds 88
names — but capacity.

## 3. Project Description: Goals, Objectives & Activities

**Goal:** Reduce risk factors and strengthen protective factors for 150
Eastside middle-school youth through sustained, structured mentoring.

| Objective | Activities | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Serve 150 youth** (up from 60) | Recruit through school partnerships; open a third site at Hillside | Enrollment roster |
| **2. Provide each youth a trained mentor** | Recruit + screen 60 new mentors; deliver 12-hour evidence-based training | Match records; training logs |
| **3. Improve school engagement** | Daily homework support; monthly attendance check-ins with school staff | Attendance & GPA data (with consent) |
| **4. Prevent justice-system contact** | SEL curriculum; conflict-resolution workshops; summer paid work crew | County referral data (aggregate) |

Programming runs Monday–Thursday, 3:00–6:00 p.m. during the school year, with a
six-week summer component that includes a paid work-experience stipend — a
component families consistently rank as the strongest reason for sustained
attendance.

## 4. Expected Outcomes

Within the grant year, RYC projects that at least 85% of enrolled youth will
maintain or improve school attendance, 80% will advance on time to the next
grade, and fewer than 3% will have contact with the juvenile justice system —
consistent with, and slightly conservative against, our three-year track
record. Outcomes are reported quarterly to the funder.

## 5. Organizational Capacity

Founded in 2016, RYC is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN on file) with an annual
operating budget of $612,000 and a full-time staff of nine. RYC has managed
two prior federal awards without a single finding in audit, maintains a formal
child-safety and background-check policy, and partners under signed MOUs with
the district and the county Department of Health. Program leadership includes a
director with 14 years in youth development and a licensed clinical social
worker overseeing the SEL curriculum.

## 6. Evaluation Plan

RYC contracts with an independent evaluator at the regional university to
conduct a pre/post design using validated instruments (the DAP developmental
assets profile) alongside administrative data. Data is collected at intake,
mid-year, and year-end; a quarterly dashboard tracks enrollment, dosage
(hours per youth), and interim outcomes so the program can adjust in real time
rather than at year's end.

## 8. Sustainability

*Bridge* is designed to outlast this grant. RYC has secured a verbal commitment
from a local family foundation to match 25% of continuation costs in Year 2,
is enrolling as a Medicaid-billable provider for the SEL component, and will
use this grant's evaluation results to pursue the district's expanded-learning
funding stream. The infrastructure funded here — trained mentors, school MOUs,
and a third site — becomes permanent capacity.
