## Why Howard's Orchard actually qualifies (most for-profits don't)

Agriculture is the big exception to "grants are only for nonprofits." Your
business hits **three funded categories at once**:

1. **Specialty crop producer** — orchards (fruit/tree crops) are the USDA's
   literal definition of a "specialty crop." ([SCBGP](https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/grants/scbgp))
2. **Value-added producer** — kettle corn, cider, jam, dried fruit, pies =
   "value-added" products made from ag commodities. This is a whole USDA grant
   program. ([VAPG](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/business-programs/value-added-producer-grants))
3. **Woman co-owned** — your wife co-owns the LLC, which (if she holds ≥50%)
   opens women-owned business grants on top of the ag grants.

The side hustles help too: window washing and kettle corn are legit small-
business lines that broaden which programs fit.

## TIER 2 — Your state's specialty-crop money (near-term)

**Specialty Crop Block Grant Program (SCBGP)** — ~$86.6M nationally in 2026.
Orchards qualify. **Catch:** you don't apply to USDA directly — you apply
**through your state Department of Agriculture**, and each state runs its own
cycle. The federal FY26 window closed June 8; the next cycle opens around
**Sept 4, 2026**. ([SCBGP](https://www.ams.usda.gov/services/grants/scbgp))

Most states ALSO run their own ag/value-added grants (often $5k–50k) with
separate deadlines. **I need your state to pull the exact programs and dates.**

## TIER 3 — The big one, worth preparing for: VAPG

**USDA Value-Added Producer Grant** is the best long-term fit for the kettle
corn / cider / jam side:
- **Planning grant:** up to **$50,000** (feasibility study, business + marketing plan)
- **Working capital grant:** up to **$200,000** (processing, packaging, marketing, inventory, salaries)
- ([VAPG program](https://www.rd.usda.gov/programs-services/business-programs/value-added-producer-grants) · [FY26 guide](https://augustbrown.com/news-item/usda-value-added-producer-grant-vapg-eligibility-application-process-examples/))

**Timing:** the FY2026 window (Feb 17 – Apr 22, 2026) has **already closed**, so
we target the **next cycle (early 2027)**. That's not a delay — it's an
advantage: VAPG requires a feasibility study and a business/marketing plan that
take *months* to do well. Starting now means we're ready when it opens instead
of scrambling. **I can draft the feasibility study and business plan** as we go.

**Also consider — REAP (Rural Energy for America):** if you'd ever add solar,
cold storage, or efficient equipment, REAP grants run **$1,500–$500k** for ag
producers. Rolling cycles. Flag me if an energy/equipment upgrade is on your
radar. ([REAP](https://augustbrown.com/news-item/usda-reap-grant-2026-complete-guide/))
