## Part 4 — Essay (DRAFT ~450 words)

**Prompt (typical): your approach to your industry, and how the funds will help
you reach your business goals.**

> Howard's Orchard is a 45-year-old family U-pick farm in Catalina, Arizona. My
> wife Ashley and I took over the orchard in 2021, and we run it as beginning
> farmers with a simple conviction: a small desert farm can still feed its
> community with clean, honest food and make an honest living doing it. On one
> pesticide-free acre of about 220 trees, we grow apples, peaches, and pears, keep
> ducks and chickens for eggs, and welcome families from across the state to pick
> their own fruit each summer and fall.
>
> Our approach to farming is low-input and community-first. We use no pesticides,
> we farm by organic practices (and are pursuing certification), and we treat the
> orchard as a place people come to — not just a place food comes from. That
> U-pick experience is our strongest advantage: our customers already know and
> trust us, which makes them the natural buyers for the value-added products we
> want to grow into.
>
> Our biggest challenge is that a one-acre orchard only sells for a few months a
> year, and a lot of good fruit — bruised or undersized "seconds" that can't be
> sold for U-pick — currently goes to waste. This grant solves both problems. A
> $15,000 New Producer Grant would fund the equipment to turn that fruit into
> shelf-stable, higher-value products we can sell year-round: a cider press,
> commercial dehydrators for dried fruit, and packaging and labeling for our jams.
> These products earn more per pound, extend our selling season well beyond the
> July–November window, and monetize fruit that is currently a total loss — raising
> our income without planting a single additional tree.
>
> Our goals over the next three years are concrete: expand our value-added line
> from jams into cider, dried fruit, and pies; complete organic certification;
> improve our irrigation to a more water-efficient, rainwater-fed system suited to
> the desert; and grow annual revenue from roughly $8,000 toward $30,000. This
> grant is the practical first step — the equipment that makes the rest possible.
>
> Above all, we are trying to keep a 45-year-old farm alive. Jim Howard started
> this orchard as "a community project," and we intend to hand it to the next
> generation stronger than we found it. An investment in our operation is an
> investment in a lasting, self-sustaining local farm.
