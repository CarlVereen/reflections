# The 5 Newsletters — Niche Selection

Selected **research-driven** from real market evidence (gathered 2026-07-16). Every
figure below traces to a source that was actually fetched or found in search. Where a
number comes only from an indexed search summary rather than a full page fetch, it is
flagged **[directional]**. Nothing here is invented — uncertainty is stated plainly.

**The pattern that won the ranking:** narrow B2B / investor / professional niches fed by
**public data streams**, where (a) buyers have money on the line, (b) paid comparables
already exist, and (c) an agent can build authority by *synthesizing public data* rather
than needing a personal brand. This is what makes an AI-written, ad-free, zero-audience
start viable.

**Economic backdrop:** vertical choice dominates economics. Per beehiiv's *State of Paid
Newsletters* (indexed summaries **[directional]**): investing newsletters carry a
~$27/mo median vs ~$7 travel; revenue per subscriber ranges ~$83 (community) to ~$230
(investing). Source: beehiiv.com/blog/how-much-money-can-you-make-from-1-000-newsletter-subscribers-in-2025.

## #1 — GovCon & Grants Intelligence  ·  *(highest-confidence pick)*

- **Reader:** business-development leads at small/mid government contractors, SBIR-seeking
  startups, grant-seeking nonprofits/researchers who need early signal on opportunities.
- **Primary money:** services / lead-gen (proposal help, capture consulting, teaming
  intros) + a $15–40/mo premium tier (curated opportunities by NAICS/agency/geo,
  deadline tracking, "who won last time").
- **Secondary money:** affiliate on registration/compliance/proposal software.
- **Willingness-to-pay (strong):** enterprise incumbent **GovWin IQ (Deltek) averages
  ~$29k/yr** (range $12k–$42k, up to $119k; entry seats ~$6k–13k) — civiciq.com,
  fed-spend.com. Mid-market: **BidNet ~$2k/yr**, FindRFP (paid DB). Grants side:
  **Instrumentl ~$299/mo**, GrantWatch (paid alerts), Terra Viva $15/yr — grantsights.com.
- **Competition:** newsletter layer is mostly free/thin (GovCon Talk Network, GovCon
  Intelligence, SmallGovCon, GovConWire). **The wedge = the gap between free newsletters
  and $29k platforms.**
- **Agent-writable:** excellent — SAM.gov opportunities/awards, Grants.gov nightly feeds,
  USAspending, SBIR.gov. All public.
- **Risks:** underlying data is public/free (must sell *curation & synthesis*, not
  access); federal budget/shutdown volatility; state/local (SLED) data is fragmented.

## #2 — Biotech / Pharma FDA Catalyst Intelligence

- **Reader:** retail & semi-professional biotech investors who trade around FDA events
  (PDUFA dates, trial readouts, AdCom meetings).
- **Primary money:** premium subscription $20–100/mo (catalyst calendar, pre-event
  setups, post-readout analysis). Highest-intent audience here — money literally rides
  on the events (investing = ~$230/sub **[directional]**).
- **Secondary money:** digital products (catalyst-tracking templates); affiliate on
  brokerage/charting tools.
- **Willingness-to-pay (strong, direct comps):** **RTT Biotech Investor $99/mo**
  (rttnews.com); BioPharmCatalyst & BiopharmaWatch (freemium paid real-time feeds);
  CatalystAlert (paid ML predictions). **Dan Sfera's free Substack has 10,000+ biotech
  investors** — proof of a large reachable free funnel (dansfera.com).
- **Competition:** the above are mostly *tools/databases* — room for a readable
  *analysis* newsletter on top. Also Benzinga/RTTNews FDA calendars.
- **Agent-writable:** very high — FDA PDUFA/AdCom calendars, ClinicalTrials.gov, 8-K/
  press releases, SEC filings.
- **Risks:** **borders on investment advice — must be framed as information/education
  with disclaimers, never recommendations.** Date accuracy is critical (errors are
  reputation-killing → the Fact-Checker gate matters most here). Lumpy event cadence.

## #3 — Cybersecurity Market & Deal Intelligence

- **Reader:** security-vendor founders/GTM, cyber VCs/PE, CISOs tracking the vendor
  landscape and budgets.
- **Primary money:** non-ad sponsor-adjacent — paid vendor "market map"/directory
  placements + lead-gen connecting vendors to buyers; premium tier / paid community for
  deal databases & budget benchmarks.
- **Secondary money:** affiliate on security tooling/training.
- **Model proof:** **Return on Security (Mike Privette)** proves the exact agent-writable
  format — weekly unpacking of cyber funding/M&A/public signals (returnonsecurity.com).
  *Honest caveat: it has moved between paid and free, so it proves the content model &
  audience value, not a clean subscription price.* High-WTP analog for the same buyer:
  **The Information — $39/mo, $399/yr, $999/yr Pro** (failory.com).
- **Competition:** free incumbents (SANS NewsBites, The Hacker News) cover *threats*, not
  *market/deal economics* — that's the gap.
- **Agent-writable:** high — public funding/M&A announcements, SEC filings, product
  launches; CISA advisories if scope widens.
- **Risks:** subscriptions look *hard* here (Return on Security's pricing flip-flops);
  money likely needs some audience first (sponsor/lead-gen). Security-savvy readers may
  scrutinize a purely AI voice — credibility bar is high.

## #4 — Vertical AI for Accounting & Finance Pros

- **Reader:** accounting-firm partners, controllers, fractional CFOs, finance-ops leaders
  keeping up with AI tools reshaping their workflow.
- **Primary money:** **affiliate = fastest first revenue** — AI tools pay generously,
  **typically 20–30% recurring, some up to 40–60%** (designrush.com). Plus digital
  products (close/audit/tax playbooks, prompt packs).
- **Secondary money:** premium tier / lead-gen to AI-implementation consultants.
- **Willingness-to-pay:** category validated top-down — a16z flagged *"The Rise of
  Vertical AI in Accounting"* (a16z.com). Finance vertical ~$20/mo median **[directional]**.
  *Caveat: I found **no** dedicated paid AI-for-accountants newsletter — this is the
  white space AND the risk (direct subscription WTP unproven; lean on affiliate/products).*
- **Competition:** free/adjacent (Accounting Today tech, a16z fintech). Generic AI
  newsletters (The Rundown 2M+, TLDR AI) are saturated but **not vertical** — go vertical.
- **Agent-writable:** very high — tool launches, funding, feature updates, public case
  studies. Fastest-moving cadence of the five.
- **Risks:** weakest *direct* subscription evidence of the five; monetize via
  affiliate/products first. Must stay tightly vertical or drown in generic-AI noise.

## #5 — Housing / Real-Estate Data Intelligence (for investors)

- **Reader:** individual/small residential RE investors and short-term-rental operators
  making buy/hold/sell calls on local market data.
- **Primary money:** low-price premium sub $7–30/mo (market-by-market data digests, trend
  calls) + affiliate on data tools (AirDNA, PropStream, lenders).
- **Secondary money:** digital products (deal-analysis spreadsheets, market reports).
- **Willingness-to-pay (proven, low-ticket):** **U.S. Housing Weekly $7/mo**;
  **RISMedia $30/mo or $249/yr** (realestatebees.com); HousingWire/Multi-Housing News
  monetize housing data at the pro tier.
- **Agent-writable:** high — Redfin Data Center, Zillow Research, FRED, Census, HUD,
  mortgage-rate feeds.
- **Risks:** low price → needs volume → slower from zero. Big free incumbents raise the
  bar → **niche down to a metro or asset class (e.g., STR, specific Sunbelt metros).**

## Ranking & "start-here" recommendation

| Rank | Niche | First-revenue lever | WTP proof | Agent-writability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | GovCon / Grants | Lead-gen + premium | Very strong (~$29k incumbent) | Excellent |
| 2 | Biotech FDA | Subscription | Strong ($99/mo comp) | Excellent |
| 3 | Cyber deal intel | Sponsor / lead-gen | Moderate (price unstable) | High |
| 4 | Vertical AI (acct) | Affiliate + products | Moderate (no direct comp) | Very high |
| 5 | Housing data | Low-price sub + affiliate | Strong but low-ticket | High |

**Rollout (decided):** "Content 5, paid 1-first" — run content for all 5 in parallel;
stand up *paid* infrastructure for a lead newsletter first, then fast-follow the rest.
Lead = **#1 GovCon** or **#2 Biotech** (strongest WTP evidence, cleanest public-data
pipeline). See `strategy/rollout.md` and `NEWSLETTER_SYSTEM.md` → roadmap.

## Rejected niches (why)
- **Generic AI news / broad consumer (travel, food, lifestyle):** saturated and/or
  lowest WTP, ad-dependent — fails the non-ad constraint.
- **Broad personal finance / stock picking:** dominated by credibility incumbents
  (Motley Fool $199–$2,000/yr); hard for an anonymous AI voice.
- **Local/city news:** almost entirely ad/sponsor-funded — fails non-ad.
- **Crypto signals:** churn, trust/scam perception, volatility.
- **Freight/logistics (SONAR):** real WTP but the paid product needs proprietary
  high-frequency feeds an agent can't source publicly — fails agent-writability.

## Uncertainties to resolve at launch (do not treat as settled)
- beehiiv per-vertical figures are indexed summaries **[directional]**, not fetched pages.
- Return on Security's current price is unclear — used as *model* proof, not price proof.
- Vertical-AI-accounting has no direct paid-newsletter comp; subscription WTP is inferred.
- Several GovCon/biotech "newsletters" are free; the paid comps are adjacent *tools*. The
  thesis (a synthesized newsletter captures value between free feeds and pricey platforms)
  is plausible but unproven — validate in Phase 0.1 before over-investing.
