# Rollout Strategy — "Content 5, Paid 1-First"

**Decision (operator, 2026-07-16):** run the free work for all 5 newsletters in parallel
from day one; stagger only the paid infrastructure — lead newsletter first, fast-follow
the rest within days once the pipeline is proven.

## Why not fully launch all 5 at once

The agent does the labor, so *labor* is not the constraint — content scales to 5 for
free. But three costs survive that are **not** about labor:

1. **Capital, spent before validation.** Each newsletter needs its own paid ESP audience
   (priced per subscriber), sending domain, payment offers, and pages. The niches are
   researched but unproven (zero paying customers). Launching 5 paid stacks = 5× upfront
   spend on 5 unvalidated bets; realistically some won't monetize.
2. **Unproven pipeline.** Nothing has run end-to-end — not the fact-check gate, not the
   ESP send, not one tool. Proving it once means fixing flaws once, then cloning a
   known-good pipeline. Launching 5 cold repeats every mistake 5×.
3. **Deliverability.** New sending domains earn reputation slowly; 5 cold domains at once
   = 5 reputations to warm and a spam problem that hits all 5 simultaneously.

## The split

| Work | Cost | Rollout |
|------|------|---------|
| Research, writing, fact-check, backlogs | none (agent labor) | **all 5 in parallel, now** |
| Web archive / SEO surface, social, audience-building | ~none | all 5 in parallel |
| ESP + sending domain + payment + paid pages | **operator $, per newsletter** | **lead first, fast-follow #2–5 within days** |

The gap between paid launches is a **validation gate** (does the pipeline produce a real
issue and a first paying customer?), not a slow queue. Expected gap: days, once proven.
