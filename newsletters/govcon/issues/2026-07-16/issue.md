# The Pentagon Just Suspended CMMC Phase II — What It Does to Your Bid Costs Right Now

*GovCon & Grants Intelligence — July 16, 2026*

## The hook

If you were about to price a cybersecurity certification into a Department of Defense bid, stop and re-read the numbers first. On July 13, 2026, the U.S. Department of War (DoW) immediately suspended CMMC Phase II — the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification requirements scheduled to take effect November 10, 2026 — and the Small Business Administration commended the move the same day ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/07/13/sba-commends-us-department-wars-suspension-cmmc-phase-ii-small-defense-contractors); effective date via [Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/cmmc-compliance/cmmc-phase-ii-suspension-dfars/)).

For a small or mid-sized defense contractor, this is not abstract policy. CMMC is the program that would have forced you to pass a third-party cybersecurity audit before you could win — or in many cases keep — work involving sensitive defense information. That gate just came off the near-term calendar. Here is what changed, what is still mandatory, and what it means for the bids in front of you this quarter.

## The body

**What the numbers looked like.** SBA analysis estimated total compliance costs reaching roughly **$593,800 per certification** for small firms that needed a third-party assessment, and about **$388,600** for firms eligible to self-assess ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/07/13/sba-commends-us-department-wars-suspension-cmmc-phase-ii-small-defense-contractors)). SBA also warned that, on its planned launch date, Phase II would have required **more than 120,000** small businesses in the defense industrial base (DIB) to seek compliance through a system supported by only **about 100 approved assessors** ([SBA](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/07/13/sba-commends-us-department-wars-suspension-cmmc-phase-ii-small-defense-contractors)). Six figures per firm against a hundred available assessors — that bottleneck is the pressure the suspension relieves.

DoW framed the decision as reducing bureaucracy while holding a security baseline in place, per reporting from [DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/07/13/dod-halts-cmmc-cybersecurity-requirements-phase-2/) and [Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/07/dod-suspends-cmmc-phase-2-launches-60-day-reform-review/414739/).

**What is paused.** The Phase II certification requirement — third-party assessments and other pending CMMC milestones in current contracts — is on hold. A **CMMC Reform Task Force** is now running a top-to-bottom review and is due to deliver a final report to the DoW CIO within **60 days** of the July 13 suspension, putting the deadline at roughly **mid-September 2026** ([Washington Technology](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/07/dod-suspends-cmmc-phase-2-launches-60-day-reform-review/414739/)). Treat that date as approximate — it is the 60-day window, not a published calendar date.

**What is still mandatory — and this is the part not to miss.** The suspension does not lift your underlying safeguarding obligations. Still fully in force ([Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/cmmc-compliance/cmmc-phase-ii-suspension-dfars/)):

- **DFARS 252.204-7012** — the contract clause requiring you to safeguard covered defense information.
- **CMMC Phase 1 self-assessments** — the self-attestation layer.
- **SPRS scores under DFARS 252.204-7019** — a current NIST SP 800-171 self-assessment score, posted in the Supplier Performance Risk System (SPRS), is still required to be considered for award.

NIST SP 800-171 Revision 2 and its 110 security controls remain the enforced standard during this interim period, applied through self-assessment and select government-led assessments ([Kiteworks](https://www.kiteworks.com/cmmc-compliance/cmmc-phase-ii-suspension-dfars/)). In plain terms: the expensive third-party audit is paused; the self-assessment paperwork that gets you onto a bid is not.

## The takeaway

**Do this now:**

1. **Don't price a third-party CMMC assessment into near-term DoD bids** that were counting on it before November 10. That cost — up to ~$593,800 — is off the table for the interim, and carrying it will make your proposal uncompetitive.
2. **Keep your DFARS 252.204-7012, SPRS score, and Phase 1 self-assessment current.** These are unchanged and still gate award eligibility. A lapsed or missing SPRS score can still cost you a bid today.
3. **Watch for the Task Force report around mid-September.** The pause is a review, not a repeal. Don't assume it is permanent, and don't dismantle the compliance work you have already done.

**Our take (analysis, not agency guidance):** the smart posture is "maintain, don't spend." The floor obligations still separate you from a no-bid; the ceiling cost just got deferred. Hold your self-assessment discipline, redirect the audit budget you were bracing for, and re-evaluate when the September review lands.
