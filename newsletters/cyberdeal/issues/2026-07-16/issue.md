# Accenture's $4.175B OT Triple-Buy: A Services Giant Builds an End-to-End Critical-Infrastructure Platform

*Cybersecurity Deal Intelligence — Issue #1 — July 16, 2026*

> Educational and analytical only. Not investment advice. We separate disclosed figures from our own analysis throughout, and label our read as "CyberDeal's take."

## The hook

The first billion-dollar-plus cyber deal of 2026 didn't come from a product vendor rolling up an adjacent category. It came from a global services and integration giant buying into an entire market layer at once. In mid-June 2026, Accenture announced agreements to take a **majority stake in Dragos** and acquire **100% of runZero and NetRise** — three operational-technology (OT) security companies — in transactions with a **combined enterprise value of approximately $4.175 billion** ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)).

If you sell, fund, or buy security for industrial and critical-infrastructure environments, this is the deal that redraws your competitive map. Here's exactly what it is — and what it isn't.

## The structure and the price

Read the number carefully. **The $4.175 billion is a combined *enterprise value* across three targets, not a clean cash purchase price** — and it covers a *majority* stake in Dragos, not a full buyout, plus all of runZero and NetRise ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)). The figure is subject to customary purchase-price adjustments, and no per-company allocation has been disclosed.

- **Not yet closed.** The transactions are expected to close in **August or September 2026**, subject to customary closing conditions including required regulatory approvals ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)). This is a live, forward-looking story.
- **Dragos stays independent.** Post-close, Dragos will continue to operate as an independent, vendor-neutral business led by co-founder and CEO Robert M. Lee, with runZero and NetRise operating under Dragos ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)).

## What each target adds

The three pieces are assembled to cover the OT stack end to end ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)):

- **Dragos** — OT threat detection, a vendor-neutral platform, and a proprietary industrial dataset. The anchor.
- **runZero** — exposure assessment and attack-surface intelligence. The "what's actually on the network" layer.
- **NetRise** — firmware-level device visibility and a software supply-chain dataset. The "what's inside the device" layer.

Detection plus exposure plus firmware and supply-chain visibility, stitched into one stack. That's the shape of a platform, not a point product.

## The ARR and market context

Together, the three companies are estimated to generate **approximately $208 million in annual recurring revenue as of June 2026, a 53% year-over-year increase** ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)).

> **CyberDeal's take:** ~$4.175B of enterprise value against ~$208M of ARR pencils out to roughly a **~20x ARR multiple**. That's our own calculation, not a disclosed figure — and a blunt one, since the EV spans only a majority of Dragos while the ARR is the full three-company total, so the true multiple on what Accenture actually bought is higher. Either way, it's a growth-multiple print for an OT category that used to trade like infrastructure plumbing.

Why pay it? Accenture frames a large, expanding runway: an estimated **~$7 billion OT cybersecurity *services* market in 2026**, sitting inside a broader OT cybersecurity market of **~$27 billion in 2026, projected to reach nearly $59 billion by 2031 (~16% CAGR)** ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/); [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)). Treat these as Accenture's cited estimates, not independent analyst data. For context on the buyer's momentum: Accenture has grown its cybersecurity business to **$10 billion of revenue in fiscal 2025, up from $700 million in 2016 — a 35% CAGR, roughly four times its overall rate** ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/06/19/accenture-dragos-runzero-netrise-acquisition/)).

This is also not Accenture's first OT move. The deal builds on more than a decade of OT-security acquisitions, including Cimation and Revolutionary Security ([pulse2](https://pulse2.com/accenture-to-acquire-majority-stake-in-dragos-and-acquire-runzero-and-netrise-for-4-175-billion/)).

## What it signals — CyberDeal's read

The buyer profile is the headline. OT and critical-infrastructure security has crossed from niche to platform-scale, and the acquirers writing the biggest checks are now global **services and integration giants**, not only product vendors. The strategic logic — pairing detection with asset/exposure and firmware/supply-chain visibility, then wrapping it in a services delivery machine — puts the independent OT and asset-visibility long tail squarely in play as consolidation targets.

## The takeaway — what to watch

- **Vendors / founders:** If you're a standalone OT-detection, asset-discovery, or firmware/supply-chain visibility company, you're now either a platform component or a competitor to one. Map where you sit against a combined Dragos–runZero–NetRise stack before your buyers do.
- **Investors:** Watch whether disclosed enterprise value equals cash-out. This is a *majority* stake in Dragos, so the headline $4.175B is enterprise value across three deals — not a single clean acquisition price. That distinction matters for read-through comps on the next OT round or exit.
- **CISOs / buyers:** Nothing changes at your renewal until the deal closes — expected August or September 2026, pending regulatory approval. Track the regulatory timeline and Dragos's promised "independent, vendor-neutral" posture post-close, since integration into a services giant is the variable that could reshape roadmap and pricing.
