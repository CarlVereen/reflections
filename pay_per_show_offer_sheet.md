# Pay-Per-Show Appointment Offer Sheet

Your model, in one line:
**You generate the lead, you *set* the appointment, and the buyer only pays
when the prospect shows up.** You never close the sale — the buyer does that
when the person walks in. This plays to your strength (setting), not your
weakness (closing).

Built around **chiropractic new-patient appointments** (your warm first
buyer via Med Masters), but the same structure works for HVAC, cleaning,
or any local service — just swap the niche words.

---

## Why this model fits you

- **You set, you don't close.** You control the outcome you're paid on
  (the show), not the outcome you can't control (their close).
- **Paid on delivery of a *show*** — not on the buyer's sales skill.
- **A booked appointment is worth 3–5× a raw lead**, so you charge more per
  unit and need fewer of them.
- **Risk-free to the buyer** = easy yes. No retainer, no contract, no risk
  to them. That's your closing lever.

---

## The offer (what you say to a chiropractor)

> "I'll book qualified new-patient appointments straight into your
> calendar. You don't pay for leads, you don't pay a retainer — you pay
> **only when the patient actually shows up.** If they no-show, it's free.
> Want to try the first few and see the patients walk in?"

That sentence does the whole job: no risk, no retainer, pay-only-on-results.

### Short pitch script (for a warm Med Masters contact)
1. **Hook:** "Quick idea for you — separate from the reactivation stuff."
2. **Offer:** "I can send you brand-new patients, booked right into your
   schedule. You only pay when they show. No-show is free."
3. **Proof frame:** "Let's do a small test — I'll book a handful, you see
   how many show and convert, and we go from there."
4. **Close (setting, not selling):** "If I get you [X] booked this week,
   are you good to see them?" → get the yes → get logistics.

## Define the terms (kills all disputes)

Put this in writing before you send a single appointment.

**A "qualified appointment" means the prospect:**
- Is in the buyer's service area / target zip codes
- Wants the service (new-patient exam / adjustment — not a job-seeker,
  not an existing patient, not a wrong-number)
- Agreed to a specific date and time on the buyer's calendar
- Gave a valid, reachable phone number
- Consented to be contacted (consent captured + timestamped)

**A "show" means:** the prospect attends the appointment (or, if you agree
to count calls, takes and completes the confirmation call). Define it once,
explicitly.

**No-show guarantee:** if a booked prospect does not show, the buyer owes
nothing for that appointment. (Optional: you re-book or replace it.)

## Pricing

Price off the buyer's economics, not your cost. One new chiro patient is
worth hundreds up front and often $1,000+ over their care plan — so a
showed appointment is cheap to them.

| Tier | Price per SHOWED appointment | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Intro / pilot | $50–75 | First buyer, proving the model |
| Standard | $75–125 | Once you have a show-rate track record |
| Premium | $125–175+ | Exclusive, high-value niches / strong show rate |

Rules of thumb:
- Keep your **ad cost per booked-and-showed appointment** well under your
  price (aim for at least 2–3× markup after no-shows).
- Start at the **low end for the pilot** to make the yes effortless, then
  raise once you can point to patients who showed.

## How the machine runs (the loop)

```
1. Buyer says yes (warm chiro via Med Masters) — no money changes hands yet
2. Simple offer + one-page form  ("New patient special — $XX exam, book online")
3. Traffic: Google Search / Local Services Ads (text-first, no design needed)
4. Lead comes in → YOU set it: call/text, qualify, book a specific time
5. Confirmation + reminders (this protects your show rate — your skill)
6. Prospect shows → buyer treats them → you bill for the show
7. No-show → free → rebook or move on
```

You are steps 4–5. That's setting. That's your edge.

---

## Protect your show rate (this is where you win or lose)

Your whole paycheck rides on shows, so treat show rate as the product:
- **Confirm fast** — set the appointment while the lead is hot.
- **Reminder sequence** — text + call before the appointment (24h + 2h).
- **Set firmly** — specific day/time, not "sometime this week."
- **Optional deposit / firm commitment** for higher-value niches.
- **Track show rate per source** so you cut what doesn't show.

## First 30 days

**Week 1 — Lock the buyer**
- Pitch 1–2 warm chiropractors from Med Masters using the script above.
- Get a verbal yes on price-per-show and the definition of "qualified +
  showed." No retainer, no contract — just the handshake.

**Week 2 — Build the machine**
- One offer, one landing page/form, one traffic channel (start with Google
  Search / Local Services Ads).
- Set up your setting workflow: instant notification → call/text → book →
  reminders.
- Capture consent on the form.

**Week 3 — Turn it on small**
- Small ad budget. Book the first handful of appointments yourself.
- Obsess over show rate, not volume.

**Week 4 — Prove and adjust**
- Count showed appointments. Bill for them.
- Show the buyer the result → raise price or add volume → repeat.
- Only after this works, consider a second buyer or a second niche.

---

## Watch-outs

- **Define "qualified + showed" in writing** or you'll argue about invoices.
- **Consent/compliance** — capture how each prospect opted in; keep records.
- **Don't over-promise volume** — under-promise, over-deliver on the pilot.
- **One buyer = fragile.** Once the model works, line up a second so you're
  not dependent on one clinic.
