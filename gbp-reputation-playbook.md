## 🚨 #1 — The compliance landmine that could sink the whole business: review gating

**The single most important finding.** The standard GHL "sentiment gate" — *"Were you happy? 👍/👎"* → route happy customers to Google, unhappy to a private form — is **prohibited by BOTH Google policy AND U.S. law.** This is the exact flow we sketched earlier in the brief. **We must not build it that way.**

- **Google policy:** *"Don't discourage or prohibit negative Google reviews or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers."* (via ReviewTrackers, quoting Google) — https://www.reviewtrackers.com/blog/review-gating-google/
- **Whitespark, verbatim:** *"'review gating' is when you ask your customers how their experience was first, then you send the unhappy customers to a feedback form and the happy customers to Google to leave you a review. Google has made this explicitly against the guidelines."* — https://whitespark.ca/google-business-profile-guide/reviews/
- **FTC rule** (final rule on consumer reviews, effective **Oct 2024**): bans fake reviews, review suppression, and incentives conditioned on positive sentiment. Penalties up to **~$51,744 per violation**; Fashion Nova paid **$4.2M** for suppressing sub-4-star reviews. `[verify on ftc.gov]`
- **Documented consequence** (Sterling Sky `[verify]`): a business caught gating had **all reviews wiped across every location and rankings cut in half overnight.**

### What we do instead (compliant AND still protective)
| ❌ Don't (gating) | ✅ Do (compliant) |
|---|---|
| Ask "happy? Y/N" then branch the ask | Send the **same** Google review request to **every** customer |
| Route unhappy → private form only | Offer an **unconditional** "tell us privately how we did" channel visible to *everyone*, that does not gate the public link |
| Pay/reward the **customer** for a positive review | Reward the **employee/tech** for *requesting* reviews (the one sanctioned incentive) |

> **Whitespark, verbatim (the legal workaround):** *"you can incentivize your employees. Reward them for requesting reviews from your customers."*

**Our real protection against bad reviews isn't a gate — it's doing great work + asking everyone (so the 5-stars bury the occasional 1-star) + expert response + removal tactics (§9).** Audit every GHL sub-account for a sentiment gate before the Google link — that's the biggest liability in this space.

## #2 — Why Google deletes *legit* reviews, and how we structurally avoid it

Owners in our research are terrified of reviews vanishing (a whole Reddit theme). Sterling Sky's *"Review Posting Blocks Are Surging"* work explains the mechanism — and it indicts the standard agency setup. `[verify]`

**The reframe:** Google flags review *behavior/pattern*, not just volume. The triggers:
- Reviews left **on the business premises / on the business's WiFi or IP** (same-IP clustering).
- **QR codes / tablets at the counter** — called out by name.
- **Clustered velocity** — many reviews at once from the same location.
- The **combination** (on-site + QR + clustered) is what gets even honest businesses hit with a ~30-day "review posting block."

### What we do differently (a real differentiator to sell)
**Push the *ask* on-site, but force the *submission* off-site and time-delayed.** Text a direct link the customer taps **later, at home, on their own device and cellular data** — hours after the tech leaves. This structurally avoids the same-IP/on-premises/clustered pattern. **Most GHL agencies do the opposite (QR at the kitchen table) — that's the thing they get wrong.**

Sources: https://www.sterlingsky.ca/review-posting-blocks-are-surging-heres-the-data-plus-the-patterns-behind-them/ · https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-reviews-not-publishing/

## #3 — Velocity & recency beat total count: meter the sends

The most under-weighted ranking truth: **the flow of new reviews matters more than the size of the pile.**

- **Whitespark 2026** treats these as *separate* ranking factors: #6 high rating (181), #9 quantity of reviews **with text** (170), #11 **recency** (164), #14 **"sustained influx over time rather than bursts"** (154). — https://whitespark.ca/local-search-ranking-factors/
- **Joy Hawkins, verbatim:** *"The number of reviews you've gotten this month matters more than your total number of reviews."* `[verify]`
- Businesses that stop getting new reviews for **~3 weeks** see measurable ranking declines. `[verify]`
- **The "first ~10" threshold:** disproportionate ranking lift getting to ~10 reviews, then diminishing returns — after that, *recency* is the lever, not raw count. `[verify]`

### What we do
**Meter requests into a smooth, indefinite trickle.** A contractor who closes 40 jobs and blasts 40 requests same-day is doing the *worst* thing — it underperforms (#14) *and* trips the spam filter (§2). We pace sends across the pipeline so the profile receives a steady drip forever. This is the operating principle of our whole review engine.

## #4 — Keywords go in the OWNER'S RESPONSE, not the review text

The juiciest counterintuitive finding, and it kills a tactic every agency (and that Reddit owner) swears by.

- **The popular belief** (Whitespark #36, BrightLocal): coach customers to say "best paver patio in Dallas." These are *survey opinions*.
- **The only controlled test on record** (Sterling Sky): keywords in the customer's review **text had NO measurable impact on Map Pack rankings** — in one case rankings got *worse*. What *did* move rankings: **keywords in the business's *response*** (Google indexes owner-response text). They *"recommend not coaching clients to include certain words in their reviews."* `[verify]` — https://www.sterlingsky.ca/does-the-text-of-a-google-review-affect-map-pack-rankings/

### What we do
1. **Never script customers.** Instead prompt for *detail* with open questions (Whitespark, verbatim): *"What service did we complete for you? Which location? Did you work with anyone specific?"* — detail naturally surfaces service/city/name keywords, converts better, and looks less filter-able.
2. **Put the target keywords in the owner response we write** — "Thanks for trusting us with your *paver patio in Plano*…" — which we control 100%, is compliant, and is where the ranking signal actually lands.
3. Reviews **with photos** hold top positions far longer (Whitespark) — we prompt for a photo with the review.

## #5 — Use SMS *and* email, timed right

- **GatherUp's measured return rates:** email **~15%**, SMS **~20%**, **both combined ~26%.** Single-channel leaves ~a third of the yield on the table. — https://gatherup.com/blog/sms-vs-email-review-requests/
- **Timing:** ask within **24–48h**; response rates fall sharply after ~24h. Two touchpoints (initial + one follow-up 3–5 days later) is the sweet spot.
- **SMS rules:** ≤160 chars incl. link; send only **8am–9pm customer local time** (TCPA compliance).
- GatherUp, verbatim: SMS wins because *"people can see and respond to texts much faster than they can with email."*

*(Note: this must be reconciled with §2 — the link is delivered by SMS/email but tapped later, off-site.)*

## #6 — The under-used ranking levers (and the myths to skip)

**Levers most contractors leave on the table (we set these):**
- **GBP "Services" (structured)** — a *proven, under-used* ranking factor. Darren Shaw, verbatim: *"I have confirmed this with my own testing. Yep, services impact rankings."* — https://www.sterlingsky.ca/services-in-google-business-profile-impact-ranking/
- **Primary + secondary categories** — #1 and #8 ranking factors. The primary category is the single biggest lever; secondary categories are routinely unset.
- **Justifications** — the bolded "Provides: …" / review-mention line under a listing in the pack. This is the *one* place review keywords pay off (occupying visual real estate + matching intent → clicks). Monitor Services so Google doesn't surface the wrong one. — https://www.sterlingsky.ca/unleashing-the-power-of-google-business-profile-service-justifications-for-maximum-impact/

**Myths we do NOT sell (they're snake oil):**
- **Geotagging photos = zero impact** — Google strips EXIF/GPS on upload. Sterling Sky's 27-location test: *"no impact whatsoever."* — https://searchengineland.com/geotagging-photos-google-business-profile-rank-453525
- **Google Posts = zero ranking movement** (441 keywords, 9 weeks). Sell as conversion/freshness, never ranking. — https://www.sterlingsky.ca/do-google-posts-impact-ranking/
- **Owner responses** rank only #122 for *ranking* — but see §7, they're a *conversion* powerhouse.

*(For our visual niche — pools/outdoor living — photos and Posts still matter a lot for **conversion**, just not ranking. We position them honestly.)*

## #8 — Suspension PREVENTION (the premium "profile protection" tier)

Owners are terrified of losing the profile (a whole Reddit theme). Most suspensions are self-inflicted and preventable:

- **Account "contamination" — the #1 hidden cause.** ~**1 in 3** suspensions trace to a *contaminated user list* (old freelancer/agency accounts on the listing), not the profile itself. Ben Fisher: simultaneous suspension across multiple listings = an *account* problem, not a profile problem. `[verify]` **→ We audit and clean the manager/user list first, before touching anything.**
- **Don't manage a client's GBP from an email you also use to make random Google Maps edits** — a flagged Maps edit can suspend every profile that account manages. `[verify]`
- **Freeze the high-risk fields.** Per Stefan Somborac (Platinum PE): name, address, phone, website, **primary category** trigger re-review. Even *secondary category* edits have triggered suspensions. Photos/hours/posts are safe. — https://localsearchforum.com/threads/avoiding-suspension-when-editing-profile.62290/
- **Wait ≥60 seconds between edits; let a new profile "marinate" a week** before adding sub-categories. Ben Fisher: rapid changes trigger suspension. — https://www.steadydemand.com/suspended-and-disabled-profiles-frequently-asked-questions/
- **Website field:** direct URL only — no redirects, no YouTube-channel link, no malware/404s (all triggers). `[verify]`
- **SAB hygiene (our niche is all SABs):** **hide the address**; keep each entity's service area non-overlapping; stay within ~2 hrs of the verification address.
- **Never use virtual offices / coworking / UPS boxes.** Joy Hawkins: *"Avoid coworking spaces at all costs — they're the landmines of local SEO."* — https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/expert-tips-for-gbp-suspensions-verification-and-local-ranking/
- **Business name = real-world name only** (no "Pool Builder Phoenix"). Name spam has no algorithmic catch — it's punished only when a competitor *reports* it (suspension in ~40% of reported cases). `[verify]`

## #9 — Reinstatement & fake-review removal (the fix-it playbook)

### Reinstatement (2026 process)
1. Sign in **as the profile owner** (correct account — critical). 2. Open the appeals tool, select the suspended profile. 3. Review the flagged policy link. 4. **Attach all evidence during submission** (single multi-file button as of July 2026). 5. Submit — **do not resubmit.** 6. If denied → **"Request additional review of a denied appeal"** (multi-week wait). — https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/google-business-profile-appeal-evidence-uploads-2026 · https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145
- **Evidence** (name/address must match profile *exactly*): business registration, license, tax cert, **utility bill** (or cell-phone bill in business name), DBA, Secretary of State link, BBB/Chamber. **SAB:** vehicle/signage photo + utility bill.
- **Pre-stage a reinstatement kit per client** before it's ever needed. Joy Hawkins: *"Have your required documents… ready before a suspension hits."* Only **~4%** of businesses have docs ready when the window opens — a huge service edge. `[verify]`
- **Counterintuitive:** tenure/reviews/ad-spend are **irrelevant** to Google. Ben Fisher: *"Because you have been in business XX years… none of this matters."* Submit facts, no emotional appeals. Never delete a suspended listing (you lose reviews/history).

### Fake reviews / extortion / competitor spam
- **Removal ladder:** Flag (⚑) → Reviews Management Tool (track status; "most processed within 3 business days") → **Appeal** (batch **up to 10** reviews at once — built for review bombs) → **escalate to the GBP Help Community** with Case ID + link + screenshot (a Product Expert like cnomo can escalate). — https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773
- **Reality:** Google only removes **policy-violating** reviews, not disputes. *"Google doesn't get involved in conflict between businesses and customers."* Don't waste time on "service was slow"; do pursue conflict-of-interest/fake, off-topic, profanity, doxxing.
- **Extortion ("pay or I keep 1-starring"):** **do not pay** (adds you to shared "payer lists"). Preserve evidence → file the **Merchant Extortion Form** (`support.google.com/business/contact/merchant_extortion`, routes to Trust & Safety) → flag each review → FTC / FBI IC3.
- **Competitor listing spam** (keyword-stuffed names, fake lead-gen listings): **Business Redressal Complaint Form** (`support.google.com/business/contact/business_redressal_form`). File **separate submissions per violation type**; frame as **fraud/consumer harm, not "my competitor"** (Google ignores turf wars).
- **Non-obvious tells** (Ben Fisher): reviewer geographically far from the business ("someone from India reviewing a California company"), one account 1-starring several businesses same day, brand-new accounts. **Ex-employee reviews:** only removable if they list your company on **LinkedIn** (that's your conflict-of-interest proof). **Define slang/regional terms** in the appeal so Google's reviewer catches the violation.
- **Mass-flagging is a myth** — Google weighs policy match, not vote count. `[verify]`
- **Never buy reviews** — Ben Fisher: buyers *"usually become the target of fake reviews and blackmailing."*

Sources: https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/how-to-remove-google-reviews/ · https://www.digitaleer.com/fake-review-extortion-how-to-fight-back/ · https://www.internetmarketingninjas.com/blog/local-marketing/how-to-deal-with-fake-reviews-on-google-business-profiles-with-ben-fisher/
