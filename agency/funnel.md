# The Funnel

Two funnels live here:
- **Funnel A — How Carl gets clients** (the agency's own acquisition funnel). This is the priority.
- **Funnel B — The website funnel we build for each client** (the product blueprint).

---

## Funnel A — How Carl gets clients

The wedge for a brand-new agency with no case studies is the **free audit / teardown**. You lead with a specific, visible gap in their online presence, hand them a personalized diagnosis, and the offer sells itself.

### Stage 1 — Target (build the list)
- Pick ONE niche (from the shortlist: dental, med spa, or high-ticket home services).
- Find established local businesses ($20–40k/mo) with a **visible gap**: weak/outdated website, unmanaged Google profile, unanswered reviews, or not ranking for their key search.
- The gap is the reason for the outreach — it makes the message specific and impossible to ignore.

### Stage 2 — Outreach (start the conversation)
- Personalized first touch (email / DM / call) leading with a **specific observation**, not a pitch:
  > "I looked at your Google profile — 4.2 stars but ~30 reviews with no response, and your site isn't showing up for '[key local search]'. Mind if I send you a quick 5-min video showing where you're losing customers? Free, no strings."
- The ask is small: permission to send a free audit.

### Stage 3 — The Audit (deliver value first)
- A **5-minute personalized video** (Loom-style) walking through their website + reputation, showing exactly where customers leak out.
- End with one CTA: **book a 15–20 min call** to walk through the fixes.
- This is the whole engine — value upfront earns the meeting.

### Stage 4 — The Call (diagnose → offer)
- Diagnose live → show the gap → show what "fixed" looks like.
- Present the four pillars as one system.
- **Founding-member close:** "I'm taking my first 5 clients at $189/mo locked for a year, in exchange for a testimonial. After that it's $497. Want one of the spots?"
- Scarcity is real (only 5), the price is a no-brainer, and the case-study framing removes the "why should I trust you" objection.

### Stage 5 — Close & Onboard
- Simple agreement: founding rate, 12-month lock.
- Collect Google/Yelp profile access. Set up hosting (we host).
- Build the conversion site + connect reputation + launch first review-generation campaign.

### Stage 6 — Retain & Expand
- **Weekly report = the retention mechanism.** Visible value every 7 days.
- Month-2 renewal = product validated.
- Once you have proof: raise to $497, ask every happy client for referrals, and qualify high-ticket clients for future Offer #2 (cold email).

### Rough volume math (to land 5 founding clients)
Work backwards from conservative rates — treat these as hypotheses to measure, not facts:
- Close rate on booked calls: ~25% → need **~20 calls**
- Audit → call booked: ~20% → need **~100 audits sent**
- Outreach → audit accepted: ~20% → need **~500 targeted outreaches**

Tighten every number with real data as you go — that's the same data-driven discipline we sell.

## Funnel B — The website funnel we build for each client

Every client site follows the same conversion architecture (this is the "funnel type architecture" we promise). One page, one job: turn a visitor into a booked customer.

1. **Hero** — Clear promise + single primary CTA above the fold ("Book a free estimate"). No confusion about what they do or what to do next.
2. **Social proof** — Reviews/ratings surfaced immediately (this is why reputation feeds the site — the good reviews we build show up here and on Google).
3. **Services / offer** — What they do, framed around the customer's outcome, not a feature list.
4. **Why us / trust** — Differentiators, guarantees, credentials, before/afters.
5. **Repeated CTA** — The same conversion action offered again at every natural stopping point.
6. **Booking / contact** — Frictionless capture (call, form, or booking widget).
7. **Follow-up hooks** — Capture the lead even if they don't convert on visit one.

**The tracking that makes it a system:** on every client site we measure where people click, whether they enter the funnel, and the conversion rate at each step — and that feeds the weekly report and the AI adjustments.

**The buyer journey we're optimizing for:** someone gets a referral or a message ("I'm Charlie, I did your neighbor's tree") → checks Google reviews → clicks to the site → the site converts them. Reputation and website work as one motion. That's the whole thesis.
