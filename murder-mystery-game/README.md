# 🥧 The Case of the Missing Blue-Ribbon Pie
### A Print-and-Play 4th of July Backyard Mystery — Family Cookout Edition
### Built for 18 players: 15 adults + 3 kids (everyone gets a character!)

It's the biggest day of the year on Maple Street: the **Independence Day
cookout** — and the crown jewel of the afternoon is the **pie contest**. For
**twelve years running**, Grandma Rose's blue-ribbon apple pie has taken first
place and the title of Maple Street Champion. This year, someone finally had
enough. During the flag salute — every hand over every heart, every eye on the
flag — a thief slipped to the dessert table and **stole the champion pie right
out from under the whole neighborhood.** No pie, no contest, no champion...
unless someone can follow the clues, break the alibis, and unmask the
culprit before the fireworks light up the sky. 🎆

Nobody gets hurt in this mystery — it's a **stolen pie**, not a "murder" — so
it stays fun, silly, and worry-free for kids. All the fun of a whodunit, none
of the scary stuff.

---

## ✨ The best part: you don't have to host it

Most murder mystery games need one grown-up to run everything — which means
that person already knows the answer and can't play. **This one is different.**

- **One character is always the thief**, and the answer lives in a sealed file
  (`99-SOLUTION-DO-NOT-OPEN.md`) that nobody opens until the end.
- **Every player only reads their own character booklet.** So even the person
  who prints and sets everything up doesn't know whodunit — you only know your
  own character's secrets, just like everyone else.
- **A read-aloud "Backyard Announcer" script runs the party for you.** Players
  take turns reading it out loud, round by round. No adult referee needed. No
  judgment calls. It just... runs.

That means **you can set it up in the morning and still play (and be surprised)
that afternoon.** Follow the spoiler-safe setup guide and you'll never see the
solution while prepping.

## 🧭 How the game works (2-minute version)

1. Each player gets a **character** (a fun cookout guest with a costume, a
   secret, and clues of their own).
2. The party runs in **5 short rounds**, guided by the read-aloud **Master
   Script**.
3. At the start of each round you **open a numbered Clue Envelope** and read
   the new evidence aloud.
4. Players **mingle and question each other — but guard their own clues** (you're
   all racing to solve it first). Everyone has a secret, so everyone acts a
   little suspicious!
5. In the final round, everyone **secretly writes their own solution** — no
   teaming up, because you want to be the one who cracks it! Then the sealed
   solution opens. **Anyone who privately solved it wins as a Master Sleuth; if
   nobody figures it out, the thief pulls off the perfect crime and wins!** 🎉

Plays in about **45–75 minutes**. Perfect for a 4th of July cookout, birthday
party, class party, or any backyard get-together.

## 👥 How many players?

This edition is built for **18 players — 15 adults + 3 kids — and everyone gets
their own suspect character.** The whole cast is family-friendly, so grown-ups
and kids play the same game together.

- **15 adult roles** (`characters/01–15`) — a whole neighborhood of quirky
  suspects, each with a secret and a motive.
- **3 kid roles** (`characters/16–18`) — easy, fun parts, and each kid holds a
  **real, important clue** that helps crack the case.
- **A few extra guests?** They join as **extra guests** with no booklet — they
  mingle, snoop, and race to solve it too. (Or two people can share a chatty
  role as "partners.")
- **A few fewer?** You can drop some of the pure red-herring adults without
  breaking the mystery — see the Character Guide for which roles to keep.
