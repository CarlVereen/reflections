# 🥧 The Pie That Wasn't
### A Print-and-Play 4th of July Backyard Mystery — Family Cookout Edition
### Built for 18 players: 15 adults + 3 kids (everyone gets a character!)

It's the 4th of July cookout and pie contest at **Maple Farm**. For years,
Grandma Rose's apple pie has taken the blue ribbon — but this year she's baked
something priceless right inside it: **Grandpa Joe's gold wedding ring**, to give
to a grandchild at the fireworks. Then, during the flag salute — every hand over
every heart, every eye on the flag — the **whole pie vanishes.** One of the guests
took it. It's up to everyone to figure out **who... and why.**

Because here's the secret at the heart of this mystery: **it was never about the
pie.** And the "why" is going to matter more than the "who."

Nobody gets hurt in this mystery — it's a **stolen pie**, not a "murder" — so it
stays warm, funny, and worry-free for kids. All the fun of a whodunit, none of the
scary stuff, plus an ending the whole family decides together.

---

## ✨ What makes this one special

- **Everyone plays a part — and everyone votes.** No lone detective. All 18 guests
  are suspects *and* investigators. At the end, every single player votes on who did
  it, then the truth comes out.
- **A real twist, not just "the sore loser did it."** The thief is the last person
  you'd suspect, and their reason will surprise you — and probably get them
  forgiven.
- **Forced-truth mingling.** Nobody can lie about the facts on their card — but they
  *can* spin an innocent reason. The whole game is asking the right questions and
  catching the excuses that don't add up.
- **A host-narrated run-of-show.** One person emcees with a read-aloud script. It's
  okay if the host knows the ending — their job is to land the twist.

---

## 🧭 How the game works (2-minute version)

1. Each player gets a **character** — a cookout guest with a costume, true facts,
   two motives, and a suspicion of their own.
2. The **host** reads the opening and the rules, then the party runs in **3 short
   mingling rounds.**
3. At the end of each round the host **reads a numbered Clue Envelope** aloud,
   steering everyone closer to the truth.
4. Players **mingle and question each other.** You must answer honestly about your
   facts — but you can spin the *reason.* Everyone acts a little suspicious!
5. Then **everyone votes** on a ballot: who took the pie, and why. The host reveals
   the answer — and everyone who named the thief is a **Master Sleuth.**
6. Finally, the whole family takes one last vote together: a **forgiveness vote**
   that gives the game its heart. 🇺🇸

Plays in about **75–90 minutes.** Perfect for a 4th of July cookout, reunion, or
any big backyard get-together.

## 👥 How many players?

Built for **18 players — 15 adults + 3 kids — and everyone gets their own suspect
character.** Grown-ups and kids play the same game together.

- **15 adult roles** (`characters/01–15`) — a whole family and neighborhood of
  quirky suspects, each with a secret and two motives.
- **3 kid roles** (`characters/16–18`) — easy, fun parts, and each kid holds a
  **real, important clue** that cracks the case. The kids are the heroes.
- **A few extra guests?** Add clue-free "neighbor" walk-ons who mingle and vote.
- **A few fewer?** Drop some of the pure red-herring adults without breaking the
  mystery — see `characters/00-CHARACTER-GUIDE.md` for which roles to keep.

> One person needs to be the **host/narrator** — they run the script and hold the
> solution. If the host also wants a character, hand the narrator duties to a
> co-host. Different group size, theme, or age range? See "Make it your own" in
> `01-HOST-SETUP-GUIDE.md`, or just ask.
